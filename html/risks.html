Risks

What are the risks that AI poses
with the spread of misinformation and Disinformation

Ai poses many risks with the spread of misinformation and disinformation. It poses these risks as it provides ordinary people with the ability to quickly both create and spread this misinformation and disinformation. Before the rise of AI (Artificial Intelligence) it took a long time to both create and make it believable, however with the rise of AI and LLM they can almost instantly create believable misinformation and disinformation. This provides ordinary people with the ability to spread it creating a large amount of disinformation being spread on social media. There are two main risks that AI poses with misinformation and disinformation, these are Deepfakes and AI algorithms in social media.

Deepfakes

AI poses a risk with the spread of misinformation and disinformation through deep fakes. As AI advances, the quality at which they make deepfakes increases. This causes deep fakes to be more believable, and so decreases the believability of videos as people are unsure if they are real or not. This is a risk as it means that people are unable to know if the videos they are watching are genuine or not.

According to (Bennett & Livingston, 2018; Chadwick et al., 2018; Flynn et al., 2017; Rojecki & Meraz, 2016; Waisbord, 2018), “Political deepfakes are an important product of the Internet’s visual turn. They are at the leading edge of online, video-based disinformation and, if left unchallenged, could have profound implications for journalism, citizen competence, and the quality of democracy.” This quote shows how deepfakes could be an issue for journalism, citizens, and democracy if left unchallenged and unregulated.

The implication of deepfakes being unregulated for journalism is that if they are not regulated, there will be less trust in journalism as people won’t be able to trust if the videos shown are real or deepfakes. This will lead to there being less consumption of news and journalism.

Citizen competence will be impacted by deepfakes if they are left unregulated, as it means that people will be more likely to do things that they normally would not do in fear of being recorded. Deepfakes give them an out with the ability to claim that it is a doctored deepfake.

The quality of democracy will be impacted if deepfakes are left unregulated as it will mean that voters are unable to tell if videos released by politicians are real or not. This will lead to less voter trust and allow corruption to occur more frequently, as politicians can say that videos showing them committing wrongdoings are deepfakes and not true. These reasons show why deep fakes are an issue in the spread of misinformation and disinformation.

An example of the risks of deepfakes was in India in April 2018: “A video goes viral on WhatsApp, the world’s most popular mobile instant messaging platform. The footage, seemingly from a CCTV camera, shows a group of children playing cricket in the street. Suddenly, two men on a motorbike ride up and grab one of the smallest kids then speed away. This “kidnapping” video creates widespread confusion and panic, spurring an 8-week period of mob violence that kills at least nine innocent people (BBC News, 2018). The footage that sparked these vendettas was a clever fake—an edit of a video from a public education campaign in Pakistan, designed to raise awareness of child abductions. The educational video opens with the kidnapping but, soon after, one of the hired actors gets off the motorbike and shows a sign cautioning viewers to look after their children. In the fake video that went viral across India, this “big reveal” device was cut; all that remained was a shockingly realistic video of a child being snatched.”

This shows how people believe things that they see on the internet without fact-checking them. This was a basic deepfake where part of the video was cut to make it look real, and as deepfakes become better, they will become more realistic, and people won’t be able to trust videos they see. This will then make people less likely to trust the media and videos they see.

We can see that if deepfakes are left unchecked and unregulated, there will be many problems with them in the future, from less trust in politicians to issues with the media.

Algorithmic Bias and Echo Cambers

AI poses a risk with the spread of disinformation and misinformation as there is algorithmic bias within social media. The AI algorithm for social media platforms is built so that it shows you the types of posts that you interact with the most so that you spend longer on the platform. This, however, is a problem with the spread of misinformation and disinformation as it means that if you are interacting with these posts, the algorithm is going to show you more of them, turning your social media feed into an echo chamber and reinforcing the misinformation and disinformation within your mind as true, making you believe that it is true.

We can see that this algorithmic bias in social media: “Through the Cambridge Analytica data scandal, the world understood the power of these tools to undermine democratic mechanisms. The political consultancy Cambridge Analytica used several million Facebook users’ data to successfully influence and manipulate public opinion in such events as the 2016 US presidential election and the 2018 Brexit referendum.” This shows how the AI and algorithmic bias can be used to spread disinformation for the 2016 US election. This shows how it can have real-world effects as this influenced the election in one of the biggest superpowers in the world and in the future, if this happens again, it can lead to very bad consequences for the countries that it happens to.

“Also, in several other elections such as those of Austria, Belarus, Bulgaria, France, Germany, and Italy, there appears to be evidence of Russia manipulating voter outcome via fake news and misinformation posted on social media.” This further proves that there is a danger as we can see that Russia is using this issue of algorithmic bias and AI to manipulate elections in European countries to their benefit. They do this by spreading misinformation on social media, which is picked up and spread by the algorithm and the people reading it, causing more people to be influenced by the misinformation and vote based on this information.

A positive of this algorithmic bias is that it allows people to plan protests and rebellions. A major example of this was the Arab Spring: “When the Arab Spring began in Tunisia, and Facebook, Twitter, and others enabled the organizing of mass protests, finally leading to dictator and president Zine El Abidine Ben Ali being forced into exile.” This shows how the social media platforms of Facebook and Twitter allowed the planning of mass protests for the Arab Spring. The Arab Spring caused five dictators in the Arab world to be overthrown. This is an example of how algorithmic bias can be beneficial as it spreads these ideas to like-minded people.

We can see how the algorithmic bias and AI can be a risk of misinformation and disinformation as it will share and spread this misinformation and disinformation with people who interact with it a lot, making them believe that it is true by constantly seeing it on their social media pages and ads.

Conclusion

We can see that AI poses risks with the spread of Misinformation and disinformation. We can see that the use of AI poses the risk of Deepfakes and the algorithmic bias in social media. Both risks help spread misinformation and disinformation around the internet and influence people's opinion as they decrease their trust on stuff they see online.
